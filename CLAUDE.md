## Working rules (Lorcan's)
- Before any task, show a checklist of steps with a predicted timeline, and tick steps off as you go.
- **Accuracy first** (as close to 100% as possible; he accepts about 1 wrong in 100 for a much higher answer rate), then speed. End goal: every chocolate recognised correctly in under ~3 s.
- The scanner decides by itself: no tap-to-confirm. Keep the short hold-over-the-chocolate scan.
- **Fair testing rule:** every change is measured on held-back data it has never seen (`training/sessions.json`: test-4, test-5 and test-6 held back; test-3 is in the gallery). Report % right when answering, % answered and the confusion list. Never keep a change that makes the held-back result worse. Rules chosen by looking at a test session must be confirmed on the next one.
- Every test session gets saved (`tests/test-N/` and in `~/Chocolate Scanner`), and the model should learn from mistakes in them.
- Client page look stays luxury and matches zchocolat.com (Cormorant Garamond + Jost, black and white, square edges, official logo and photos). New testing tools go on the admin page only. Everything runs on the phone.
- After changing app files, bump `VERSION` in `app/sw.js` (and `MODEL_VERSION` only if a model file changed). After editing `app/index.html`, run `python3 tools/build-admin.py`.
- Run `tools/check.sh` (add `--browser` for the browser test) before Lorcan pushes. Commit each stage separately with a clear message.
- English first; French later if needed.

- **MobileNetV3-Small backbone swap, tried and blocked (30 Oct):** the object-recognition research flagged this as a possible way to make the shared backbone (used by the reader, chocoprint and orientation models) smaller and a touch faster — not more accurate. Before committing the ~3.5 hours of laptop time a real retrain of all three models would take, checked first whether a working MobileNetV3-Small backbone could even be produced in the old file format this project's code loads (`tf.loadLayersModel`, the same format `app/vendor/mnv2` is in). It can't, two different ways: Keras's standard MobileNetV3Small compiles its hard-swish/squeeze-excite math down to an op (`TFOpLambda`) the phone-side TFJS library doesn't know how to load at all. Stopped there rather than hand-building the whole architecture from scratch to work around a third-party tool's bug, since the win on offer was a smaller download, not better answers. No training time was spent — this was caught at the first, cheap step.

- **Camera tilt (viewing angle, not rotation):** confirmed weak, with real evidence now (28 Oct), and now also
  confirmed hard to fix cheaply (29 Oct - see "Tilt-correction attempt" above). What first looked like a separate
  "N°5 magnet" problem in test-6 (zdark, N°1, N°8 and N°21 all wrongly matching N°5's chocoprint) turned out, on
  inspecting the actual saved photos, to be four badly angled, near-side-on shots — not an N°5 reference problem
  at all. Only handles roughly-overhead shots well. Tilt augmentation for the chocoprint (train a model to be
  tilt-invariant from examples) and, now, two different code-only correction attempts (mild shear guesses; real
  quadrilateral detection and unwarp) have all been tried and rejected — not currently used. Across 87 real test
  photos, neither correction attempt ever once fixed a wrong answer, while both added real risk to already-good
  photos — evidence the genuinely bad angled shots may be physically self-occluded (same reason Face ID fails at
  extreme head poses), not just mis-processed. The realistic goal remains "any angle a person could still read it
  at by eye", not literally any angle. Next lever, if picked up again, needs either a genuinely different method
  (not a variation on the two already tried) or a proper angled-photo dataset to check against - test-6's four
  real examples (`tests/test-6/closeup/{zdark,1,8,21}/`) were enough to catch an actively broken corner-detector,
  but not enough to tell whether any fix was actually working, versus just not breaking things. A gyroscope-based
  tilt hint shipped 3 Nov (see above) — it's a real-time nudge to avoid bad angles in the first place, using the
  phone's own sensor instead of guessing from the photo, but it doesn't change what the recognizer can handle once
  a frame is already tilted, and the 25° nudge threshold is still unvalidated against real sessions.
- Lookalikes: 20 ↔ 21/23/24 — the shell-logic veto that caused this is fixed (28 Oct); the underlying weakness remains (the reader still can't reliably read N°20's milk+white shell, we've just stopped trusting that reading for this one chocolate). A genuine N°21-labelled-as-N°20 mislabel in the gallery was also found and fixed (29 Oct, see "Gallery audit" above) — worth a fresh test session to see if this pair specifically improved. 10/11 and N°21 improved by the 28 Oct reference round (confirmed via before/after check). 13/19, 6/9, 12/22, 18/15/23 still open.
- Real training photos come mostly from one table and light; the 28 Oct reference round adds window + lamp light for every chocolate Lorcan has, which should help generalise beyond that one table.
- The three z-logo chocolates (dark, milk, white): the two dark-looking ones are hard to tell apart from above. zmilk specifically has never appeared in any test or reference session yet.
- MAX_GLARE (0.03) is completely unvalidated — no real test frame has ever shown any glare to check it against, across 6 sessions now.

## Next steps
1. **Validate glare** (MAX_GLARE=0.03): still never triggered across 6 sessions. Needs a deliberate, successful attempt — check in person what actually produces a strong enough reflection on a chocolate shell before assuming the threshold itself is wrong.
2. **zmilk**: still never appeared in any test or reference session. Capture it when possible.
3. **Tilt / "scan from any angle"**: two code-only approaches tried and rejected 29 Oct (see "Known weak spots" and "Tilt-correction attempt" above) - neither helped, even after fixing a real bug in one of them. A gyroscope nudge hint shipped 3 Nov as a first, cheap mitigation (prevent bad angles rather than recover from them) - needs real sessions to check whether the 25° threshold actually helps or needs tuning. The best-grounded next lever beyond that, not yet built: teach `training/synth.py` to render from a genuinely oblique virtual camera (real perspective + occlusion + shading from its existing height-map model, not the current flat 2D squash) - informed by a real precedent (a 2019 paper on reading embossed marks on 3D-printed objects from varied angles via synthetic training). A real engineering task, not a quick tweak; worth a proper go/no-go estimate before starting.
3a. **Lookalike tie-breaker** (20↔21, 6/9, 12/22, 18/15/23 - see "Known weak spots"): not yet tried - when the top two chocoprint candidates are close and happen to be a known lookalike pair, compare the straightened scan directly against the exact official artwork (`designs/mark_XX.png`, already in the repo) as a targeted final check, rather than relying only on the learned embedding. Bounded, standalone, doesn't touch the main pipeline.
3b. **Wider MobileNetV2 backbone** (see 2 Nov entry above): MobileNetV2 at width 1.4 converts and loads cleanly as a normal fine-tunable model, zero new format risk, and beat MobileNetV3 on a quick frozen-feature check. A modest, safe option worth a real fine-tuned retrain + `fair_check.sh` comparison once there's time, though not expected to be a big lever on its own.
3c. **Lower-confidence leads, not recommended yet**: distilling a larger model (DINOv2) into a small mobile backbone (mixed evidence elsewhere - helped in one mobile few-shot challenge, didn't in a separate medical-imaging one); MobileOne as an alternative backbone (strong published on-device numbers, but no confirmed path to TensorFlow.js yet - would need its own conversion-feasibility check first, same caution MobileNetV3 taught us). Don't commit real time to either without a cheap feasibility check first.
4. Nico demo feedback: client experience with a box and QR card in the existing card format; rework from his comments or make the client side ready.
5. QR card: looks like the current card but makes clear you can scan the chocolates; luxurious and simple.
6. Hooks (built, switched off for clients in `app/hooks-config.js`): welcome offer, My tasting (favourites), corporate report + thank-you. Need Nico's PrestaShop module — see the Hooks plan linked below.
