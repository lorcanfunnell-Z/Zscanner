## Working rules (Lorcan's)
- Before any task, show a checklist of steps with a predicted timeline, and tick steps off as you go.
- **Accuracy first** (as close to 100% as possible; he accepts about 1 wrong in 100 for a much higher answer rate), then speed. End goal: every chocolate recognised correctly in under ~3 s.
- The scanner decides by itself: no tap-to-confirm. Keep the short hold-over-the-chocolate scan.
- **Fair testing rule:** every change is measured on held-back data it has never seen (`training/sessions.json`: test-4, test-5 and test-6 held back; test-3 is in the gallery). Report % right when answering, % answered and the confusion list. Never keep a change that makes the held-back result worse. Rules chosen by looking at a test session must be confirmed on the next one.
- Every test session gets saved (`tests/test-N/` and in `~/Chocolate Scanner`), and the model should learn from mistakes in them.
- Client page look stays luxury and matches zchocolat.com (Cormorant Garamond + Jost, black and white, square edges, official logo and photos). New testing tools go on the admin page only. Everything runs on the phone.
- After changing app files, bump `VERSION` in `app/sw.js` (and `MODEL_VERSION` only if a model file changed). After editing `app/index.html`, run `python3 tools/build-admin.py`.
- Run `tools/check.sh` (add `--browser` for the browser test) before Lorcan pushes. Commit each stage separately with a clear message.
- English first; French later if needed.

- **MobileNetV3-Small backbone swap, tried and blocked (30 Oct):** the object-recognition research flagged this as a possible way to make the shared backbone (used by the reader, chocoprint and orientation models) smaller and a touch faster — not more accurate. Before committing the ~3.5 hours of laptop time a real retrain of all three models would take, checked first whether a working MobileNetV3-Small backbone could even be produced in the old file format this project's code loads (`tf.loadLayersModel`, the same format `app/vendor/mnv2` is in). It can't, two different ways: Keras's standard MobileNetV3Small compiles its hard-swish/squeeze-excite math down to an op (`TFOpLambda`) the phone-side TFJS library doesn't know how to load at all. Its "minimalistic" variant (plain ReLU, no squeeze-excite, built to dodge exactly that problem) does load, but the conversion tool itself silently drops every single BatchNorm layer's weights in the process (136 of 170 missing) — a real bug in the converter, not a settings mistake. Stopped there rather than hand-building the whole architecture from scratch to work around a third-party tool's bug, since the win on offer was a smaller download, not better answers. No training time was spent — this was caught at the first, cheap step.
- **Continued 2 Nov, properly tested this time before building anything:** found a different TFJS file format — "graph model" instead of the old "layers model" — that CAN load the full MobileNetV3Small variant, hard-swish/squeeze-excite and all; confirmed it loads and predicts correctly (`tf.loadGraphModel`, 42ms for a batch of 2). That looked like it unblocked the swap for real. But a graph model can only run predictions — TFJS can't fine-tune one, unlike today's `app/vendor/mnv2` backbone. Checked why that matters before spending the ~3+ hours a full pipeline rewrite (new training scripts, a shared-backbone live app, a full retrain) would take: all three real training scripts (`train_reader.js`, `train_print.js`, `train_quarter.js`) deliberately fine-tune the *entire* backbone after a short warm-up, not just a small head on top — that's the actual recipe, not an accident. So before building, ran two cheap checks on the same 85 held-back test-4/5/6 photos instead of the full rewrite:
  - Frozen MobileNetV2 0.35 (today's backbone, un-fine-tuned) + a small trained head: 17/85 right (20.0%).
  - Frozen MobileNetV3-Small-full (the new graph model) + the same small head: 25/85 right (29.4%) — genuinely better raw features, as the research predicted.
  - The real, currently-deployed reader (MobileNetV2 0.35, actually fine-tuned, no shortcuts): 44/85 right (51.8%).

  Fine-tuning is worth +32 points over a frozen backbone; swapping to the better frozen backbone was only worth +9. Since the only working way to use MobileNetV3-Small-full in TFJS (the graph-model format) can't be fine-tuned at all, switching to it would trade away a much bigger gain for a much smaller one — very likely a net loss, not a win, despite MobileNetV3's raw features testing better in isolation. **Rejected on real numbers, not just a format problem this time. MobileNetV2 0.35 stays the backbone, fine-tuned per model exactly as today**; nothing in the live app or training pipeline changed. Would need either a size-matched MobileNetV3 variant that's actually fine-tunable (none converts cleanly today) or a different method entirely before this is worth revisiting.

- **General-project research, then a gyroscope tilt hint shipped (3 Nov):** asked to look wider than just the MobileNetV2/V3 backbone question — "how to make this work definitively" overall. Found a likely reason the tilt-correction attempts above kept failing, not just another thing to try: `training/synth.py`'s synthetic photos and the rejected chocoprint `TILT` experiment (`training/lib/view.js`) both only ever fake tilt as a flat 2D squash/keystone warp of an already-overhead image — never genuine perspective foreshortening or self-occlusion. That, plus a real precedent (a 2019 paper on reading embossed marks on 3D-printed objects from varied camera angles via synthetic training) pointed at the best-grounded next lever for tilt specifically — see Next steps.
  Built the cheapest, most direct idea first: the iPhone already knows exactly how tilted it's being held, for free, via its own gyroscope — no need to guess tilt from the picture at all. On tapping "Scan a chocolate", the app now asks iOS once for motion access (same pattern as the existing camera prompt), reads the live tilt angle (`DeviceOrientationEvent`'s `beta`/`gamma`, both ~0 degrees when the phone lies flat — exactly how it's held to look straight down at a chocolate), and nudges the on-screen hint ("Hold the phone flatter...") past 25 degrees off-flat, both while holding and in the existing "stuck" message. Every held frame (and rejected one) now logs its tilt reading too, including in admin test traces, so a future session can check with real sensor data whether tilt actually predicts wrong answers and what threshold is worth using - the 25 degree cutoff is a first guess, not yet validated. **Advisory only: never blocks a frame or an answer**, same caution as not deploying an unvalidated hard gate (cf. 3b. **Wider MobileNetV2 backbone** (see 2 Nov entry above): MobileNetV2 at width 1.4 converts and loads cleanly as a normal fine-tunable model, zero new format risk, and beat MobileNetV3 on a quick frozen-feature check. 3d-ii. **Blind vision-chat review of all 28 designs (3 Nov evening, fresh Claude chat, zero knowledge of this project's own findings - genuinely independent):** sent one real close-up photo of every chocolate (from the existing reference-round captures) plus the official design sheet. Photos were renamed to hide the ground truth before identification, then unmasked to score - a proper blind test. **20/28 correct, 2 abstentions (sensible "I can't tell" rather than a wrong guess), 6 wrong.**
   **The one finding that matters most: every single error or abstention was on a blurry, underexposed or badly-framed photo - all 18 sharp, well-lit photos were called correctly at high confidence.** Design confusability only ever showed up as the tie-breaker on already-degraded photos, never on a clean one. Strong, independent evidence that the existing quality gates (MIN_SHARP/MIN_LIGHT/MIN_SIZE in app.js) are the real lever, more so than chasing individual lookalike fixes - worth a fresh look at whether those thresholds are still well-calibrated, especially MIN_SHARP given ~10 of 28 photos here had enough blur to cause a miss.
   **New confusable-design clusters, found independently (not from looking at this project's own history):** 10/11/17 flagged as the single hardest cluster on the sheet (thin italic diagonal stems, same ring-gap position); 12/13/22/23 (2 vs 3 differ only in the lowest-contrast part of the glyph); 14/17; Z misread as 2 or 7 when its top bar reads weak, especially on dark shells; a reframed 6/9/0/5 cluster with a real explanation (6's tail becomes its own enclosing ring, losing the normal digit/frame boundary entirely at low resolution, and 9/0/5 collapse toward it). **Independent confirmation of existing work:** 18 vs 19 flagged as the closest pair within the 16/18/19/15 group - matches the already-documented 18/15/23 lookalike cluster, found via a completely different method (reasoning about shapes vs this project's own geometric IoU measurement).
   Full write-up saved at `experiments/vision-chat-test-2026-11-03/emboss-id-brief.txt` (gitignored, local only, same as the rest of that folder). Also suggests: a blind eval harness (rename-then-score, exactly as done here), synthetic augmentation rendered from the vector design sheet for a from-scratch classifier, and a staff capture-guidance note (raking side light especially helps dark shells, fill the frame, hold still).
